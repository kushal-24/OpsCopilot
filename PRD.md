## WHAT MY PROJECT IS?

Imagine a big company — say a bank's back-office team, or a customer-support team. Every day, work flows through them: a request comes in, someone reviews it, it gets approved, it goes to another desk, it gets closed. Every one of these steps quietly gets recorded in their software as a log — a row saying "Task #4471 moved from *Review* to *Approved* at 2:14pm by Person X."

Now, the company *thinks* their process works one way ("requests get handled in 2 days"), but the logs often tell a different story ("actually 30% of requests get stuck at the Review step for a week"). Nobody has time to read millions of log rows to find this out. That gap — between how work is *supposed* to flow and how it *actually* flows — is exactly what IBY's ProcMine attacks.

**Your project is a small, friendly version of that.** 
Call it something like **"OpsCopilot"** — an assistant that a non-technical manager can talk to about their operations.

Here's the experience you're building. A manager uploads their operational data (or you seed it with realistic sample data). They see a clean dashboard: how long tasks take, where things get stuck, which steps are slow, any unusual spikes. Then — the special part — there's a chat box where they can just *ask questions in plain English*:

- "Why did approvals slow down last week?"
- "Which step is the biggest bottleneck?"
- "Show me tasks that took way longer than normal."
And the assistant actually answers — not by making things up, but by *going and querying the real data*, running the numbers, and replying in plain language a non-technical person trusts.

That's the whole soul of it: **turn messy operational logs into clear, trustworthy, conversational insight for someone who can't read SQL.** That is *precisely* the sentence IBY uses to describe their own company. When a reviewer opens your README, they should feel a little jolt of "wait, this kid gets what we do."
